Medians of the measured runs. CPU is milliseconds per command, from each container's cgroup counter over the whole bench command, divided by every command it sent, the warm-up's included (sent_total); engine CPU spent catching up afterward is in the raw results. Statements and commits per command are divided the same way, and count everything the run's database executed in that window, the engine's reads and rules included. Catch-up is the time from the end of the bench command until every reader reached the head as it stood at that end; events written afterward do not count. Rule latency and register lateness are in milliseconds. Transactions are those the bench driver reports committing; in a bulk workload, latency is per transaction, that is, per chunk. Throttled is the percentage of each container's scheduler periods in which it hit its CPU limit; a container throttled for much of the run was capped by its limit. Host probe is the median of five timings of a fixed CPU-bound statement in the run's database just before the bench, outside the measured window; a probe well above the night's usual marks a run during which the machine itself ran slow. Steal is the share of the kernel's CPU time taken by the hypervisor for other tenants over the bench command, from /proc/stat as the database container sees it. WAL is the write-ahead log written per command over the bench command, with full-page images per command; the WAL, checkpointer, background writer, and per-table update and vacuum counts are in the raw results. Engine ready is how long the engine took to finish starting, judged by its CPU, before the probe and the bench began.
